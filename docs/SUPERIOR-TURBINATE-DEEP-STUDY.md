## 2. Comments — what the audience actually loves

1,831 comments fetched (ep7: 592, ep6: 410, ep9: 388, ep1: 192, ep10: 249). Near-universal praise, almost no hate.

- **Yara's monologues are the emotional hook.** The top-liked comment on the biggest episode is about feeling comforted every time Yara speaks. Variants repeat everywhere: a conversation with Yara could fix me, Yara's wisdom cured my Friday, I need Yara as a friend/therapist, #YARAGANG, book recommendations from the blonde girl. The sincere lecture is what people LOVE — the jokes are what they share. The monologue makes it shareable as "smart"; the punchline makes it shareable as a joke. Both halves are doing work.
- **People quote exact lines back.** The writing is quotable because it is specific: the exact car year, the pacifier line, the threshold line, the closing line of episode 1. Quoted-back lines are almost always Yara's lectures or the title phrase.
- **Adult Swim pickup is begged for constantly.** "Full length adult swim series", "prime Adult Swim", "needs to be on Adult Swim — YESTERDAY", the Newgrounds-to-Adult-Swim comparison, "best series of 2026", "my favorite show on the internet right now", "I'm addicted to this show". The audience itself places the show on Adult Swim — the same network Cape Jones targets.
- **Style comparisons:** 90s MTV (Daria, Downtown), magazine-cutout collage, Xavier: Renegade Angel, Class of 09, Broad City. Called a "generational reincarnation of Daria" and a "macabre Bratz Doll / Daria aesthetic".
- **Puerto Rican / Boricua representation is named as a draw** — viewers caught the coqui (tree-frog) chirp at the end of episode 1 and named Yara as the representation they needed.
- **Anti-AI handmade praise.** Viewers explicitly contrast it with low-effort, soulless, AI-generated content, and say the audience shares blame when genuinely good shows don't get attention.
- **Discovery is cross-platform.** Commenters say they found it on Instagram or TikTok and came to YouTube to binge and rewatch. "Here from Instagram", "found via TikTok binging", "my gf put me on to this series".
- **The asks, in order of frequency:** longer episodes / a full series / Adult Swim; release the music (Spotify, streaming, playlist); merch ("merch when???", "How do I fund you!!!"); more Yara; specific episode ideas.
- **Criticism is rare and mild:** one "low effort? AI?" complaint on ep9 (the most-liked criticism in the whole study), one bot-skeptic on ep6, one pacing note on ep7, one "not my fav episode" on ep8.
- **The creator barely talks in comments.** No pinned creator comment on any of the five episodes checked, no creator reply in any top-20 list. The work does the talking; the one creator reply found anywhere was a Discord invite on Patreon.
- **Still moving:** episode 10 gained about 200 views during the browser session; the channel grew 35.5K to 36.1K subscribers in one day.

## 4. Craft re-watch — the timing pattern (ep7 + ep6, timed from the videos)

**Ep7 (2:27):** cold open mid-conversation, no title card. The title phrase lands as a joke at ~0:06. Beat map: found weed in a puddle → lecture about standing up for yourself → deadpan diagnosis → stunt gag → going faded on spiked SunnyD → ruling-class monologue vs. rebuttal → paranoia rant held on a Yara close-up → final punchline, title phrase as the last line, HARD CUT to black. Roughly 7–9 setups in 147 seconds, holds of 10–25 seconds, average ~15–20 seconds. Cuts land on speaker changes and on the two visual gags.

**Ep6 (1:56):** opens with a ~25-second sung intro over a SINGLE front-on shot of the car driving — the longest single hold in either episode. Beat map: car dialogue → picking up the little cousin → the blind-bags lecture held ~28 seconds in one shot → clay payoff → HARD CUT. Only 4–5 setups in 116 seconds, average hold ~25–30 seconds.

**Shared pattern:** very long static holds; cuts on speaker change or on a visual gag; punchlines delivered as a line followed by a beat of silence and a held reaction shot — NO sting, NO music bed under the dialogue; abrupt hard-cut endings that land the title phrase or a profane button line as the last thing heard.

## 5. The honest tension — what NOT to copy

$UPEREGO's animation IS long holds (15–30 seconds) and mouth flaps. Cape Jones's owner REJECTED exactly that: the 90-second CJ slideshow was "Microsoft Word level slideshow bullshit", and the standing bar is drawn changes on twos (12 drawings/sec at 24 fps), real inbetweens, Metalocalypse-level — not holds, not camera zooms on stills (see `docs/SHOW-PRODUCTION-STATUS.md` §3–4).

So: copy their PACKAGING + WRITING + FUNNEL + CADENCE. Meet Cape Jones's own animation bar INSIDE the short format. A 90-second short at 12 drawings/sec, in one location, with few setups, is achievable where an 11-minute episode isn't yet. The shorts lane is where their proven packaging meets our proven standard — not a lowering of the bar.

## 6. The Cape Jones plan (built on the repo's actual bible + status)

The show, from `docs/SHOW-BIBLE.md`: a washed-up cosmic cape-hero keeps "saving" a town that never asked him to, and fails upward because he cannot stop performing the hero part. Flaw engine: wants glory → attempts a heroic feat → succeeds at the wrong thing (or fails at the right thing) → gets the credit anyway → tag undercuts him. Format 11-minute; tone Gumball (cosmic stakes handled like a parking dispute); action spine Teen Titans 2003. Cast slots: Straight-Man Sidekick, Rival Hero, Fan (a kid), Nemesis, The Cape (the most competent character on the show), The Authority. World: a mid-size town that treats cosmic events as municipal problems; HQ is a decommissioned water tower. Signature visual: dynamic colored lighting — the blaster's light BECOMES the scene lighting while it fires. Pupil canon: white eyes by default, cartoony pupils sometimes for emphasis, rig toggle. Canon gags: the LED remote (works everywhere, logic be damned, ~30% of screen time), the light-flicker couch gag, cape-got-there-first. Eight premises already written (parade/meteor, nemesis podcast, sidekick auditions, merch where the cape outsells him, day off, catchphrase in another language, hero-duel, retire).

The owner's DNA (2026-11-21): Aqua Teen Hunger Force + JoJo's Bizarre Adventure (universe languages only) + Space Ghost + Metalocalypse — really about Cape Jones doing nothing, mundane bullshit.

**The mapping:**

1. **Shorts lane.** 1–2 minute episodes, cold open, one location, hard-cut ending, title phrase or button line last. Same shape as $UPEREGO, Cape Jones's content.
2. **Lock the trio tension from the bible's own slots.** Jones (wants glory) + the Sidekick (sees every failure, never believed) + the Fan (the kid who thinks Jones hung the moon) — or Jones + the Rival (actually competent, gets no credit) + the Nemesis (petty, inflated into an arch-villain). Lock the triangle so a viewer learns the show's argument in one short.
3. **The sincere beat.** $UPEREGO's Yara monologue is the shareable-as-smart engine. Cape Jones's equivalent is the Fan or the Sidekick's clear-eyed truth — one sincere beat per short, then the tag undercuts it. That pairing is what gets shared twice: once as smart, once as funny.
4. **Canon gags as the quotable bits.** LED remote, couch flicker, cape-got-there-first — the recurring bits viewers quote back, the same job Yara's lines do.
5. **One hero shot per short.** The dynamic colored lighting shift — the blaster's light taking over the scene — rationed to the punchline, the same way their Matrix-code shot is rationed.
6. **Languages.** JoJo universe languages plus the catchphrase premise (a catchphrase in another language he can't pronounce) — the multilingual layer the owner named.
7. **Packaging.** One-sentence premise description, same social block, same hashtags from day one (they added hashtags at ep5 and grew — start with them), no subscribe-begging, Patreon a week early, TikTok as discovery and YouTube as the binge home, soundtrack releases (their commenters beg for the music).
8. **Cadence.** Biweekly Fridays.
9. **Tools.** Harmony is the owned tool — the Linux build exists, it is not installed, and the unblocker is documented in `docs/SHOW-PRODUCTION-STATUS.md` §5. $UPEREGO's only named tool is Procreate for early work — a data point, not a recommendation. No tool is claimed without evidence; the pipeline decision stays Harmony's Linux build plus the vendored open tools, per canon (`docs/CANON-MASTER-SHOW.md`).
10. **The test short: THE PARKING LOT** (owner's favorite LED remote scene, bible §8). Night, parking lot, Jones takes out the remote without a word, hits it, the whole night lighting shifts to broken-streetlight purple, "ah, that's better." One location, one gag, one hero lighting shift. It proves the pipeline in the $UPEREGO shape: dialogue in the owner's voice, drawn changes on twos, dynamic lighting, hard-cut ending. PROPOSAL ONLY — no build started; the owner's call.

**What NOT to copy:** 15–30 second static holds; teen drug/sex/taboo content; the school setting; photobash as the only look (test it as a shorts option, not the main look); the solo "everything else by me" model — Cape Jones is a team pipeline under the single-pipeline rule.
