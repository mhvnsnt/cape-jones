## 1. The team, correctly counted — 5 confirmed + the sixth question answered

The core Ginyu Force is FIVE:
1. Captain Ginyu — leader, centre of the formation.
2. Jeice — second-in-command, elegant/refined.
3. Burter — "fastest in the universe", speed/flow.
4. Recoome — most flamboyant, power/showboat, dance-trained as a child.
5. Guldo — smallest, compact/low, concentration/time power.

Mars said "like six of them" in a voice message. He was not miscounting
something that does not exist: there IS a sixth figure, and it is documented.
**Bonyu** — created by Akira Toriyama, revealed by Bandai Namco in August
2019 for Dragon Ball Z: Kakarot — was **formerly the sixth member** of the
Ginyu Force, the only female member, same species as Jeice. She voluntarily
withdrew because she found the special fighting pose too embarrassing /
hated posing, and appears in Kakarot only as a training opponent in a
simulation side-quest. She is NOT part of the classic five-man team pose.
So: **5 core + 1 former = 6 figures total in this study**, and the
roll-call sequence below uses the canonical five in intro order, with
Bonyu kept as a labelled former-member coda, never folded into the team
pose as if she were still on it.

## 2. The team system — how the posing works as a system

- Origin of the system: a parody of Japanese sentai / tokusatsu teams
  (Toriyama, reported as watching sentai with his children): five
  colour-coded warriors, shared uniforms, roll call, synchronized poses.
  The joke that makes it work is commitment — dangerous fighters who
  treat choreography as part of the mission, dead serious about a
  ridiculous pose. That commitment rule is borrowed whole: Cape's
  Ginyu dialects are held dead-serious, never winked at mid-pose.
- Roll-call order (canonical intro order, from the team-pose sources):
  **Recoome → Burter → Jeice → Guldo → Captain Ginyu (last, centre)**.
  Each member introduces himself with his own pose, then all five shout
  the team name in unison and strike a final pose as a unit. The classic
  formation, left to right, is typically Recoome, Jeice, Ginyu (centre),
  Burter, Guldo — the roll-call ORDER and the formation ORDER are
  different things; both are recorded here so they are not confused.
- Team-pose-as-unit rules: (a) one shared beat grid — announce (hold
  12f) → snap to pose (2f) → hold (12f+), the same timing used for the
  earlier Ginyu gag dialect; (b) staggered depth — centre forward,
  flanks slightly back, so five silhouettes read as one composition,
  not five separate figures; (c) each member's silhouette must read
  alone AND in the group; (d) a missing member breaks the pose — the
  team itself worries about this in-story (four-person pose, then the
  two-survivor pose), which is why a solo performer borrowing one
  member's pose must borrow the LANGUAGE, not pretend to be the team.
- Solo borrowing rule: Cape cycling through the members' poses, one at
  a time, is a roll-call SEQUENCE, not a team. He is one performer
  doing five dialects in order. No member is impersonated: no member's
  body, colours, armour, or name is put on Cape. The dialect names
  (GINYU-CAPTAIN etc.) are production labels for pose language only.

## 3. Per-member pose language (what is distinct, and the alternatives)

### GINYU-CAPTAIN — commanding, chest thrust, S-curve, centre
Distinct: proud, confident presentation; strong S-curve body; chest
thrust forward and up, back arched, knees deeply bent, stable base;
one arm extended, one arm across the chest or on the hip. The pose is
the centrepiece — it has to read as leadership from silhouette alone.
- CAP-01 grand entrance / roll-call: left arm up-out (-100), right arm
  across to hip (90), wide legs (-40/35). The roll-call centre pose.
- CAP-02 body-change spread: BOTH arms spread wide up (-150/150),
  legs spread (-35/35) — the arms-outstretched preparation for the
  signature swap technique, rendered as pose language only (no beam,
  no effect, no second body).
- CAP-03 crossed command: arms crossed over the chest (-20/20 upper,
  110/-110 lower), legs planted (-20/20) — the windup half of the
  spread: arms cross, then open. Left arm is top-layered so the cross
  reads as one continuous chain, not eaten by the chest.

### GINYU-RECOOME — flamboyant power, wide, dance-trained, showboat
Distinct: the most pose-happy member, poses more in battle than the
captain; wide, spread legs; hands on hips or spread; power-first
silhouette; dance training shows as rhythm in the holds — poses are
performed, not just struck. Aggression is theatrical, not tense.
- REC-01 power showboat wide: arms spread up-out (-120/120), legs
  widest of the set (-50/45) — the signature wide power pose.
- REC-02 eraser windup lean-back: head back (-12°), arms drawn in
  (60/-60), legs (-30/30) — the mouth-blast windup as staging: lean
  back, gather, then fire (the blast itself is NOT drawn; this is the
  windup pose only).
- REC-03 victory taunt, hands on hips: arms to hips (120/-120),
  hip-out legs (-45/20), head high (12°) — the after-the-hit mock:
  chest out, chin up, already celebrating.

### GINYU-BURTER — speed, lean, flowing, aerodynamic
Distinct: everything leans and flows; the body is an arrow. Poses arch
and stretch to sell speed even at rest; legs stride, arms pump or
sweep; the silhouette is long and diagonal, never square. Speed is the
whole read — if a Burter pose reads static and square, it has failed.
- BUR-01 speed lean dash: deep stride (-55/15), one arm swept back
  (-140), one arm pumping forward (60/-90) — the dash frame.
- BUR-02 hurricane spin windup: arms horizontal (-90/90, 0/0 lower),
  legs (-35/35) — the spin/hurricane preparation: arms out like a
  rotor, body centred, about to turn.
- BUR-03 mach kick stance: one leg kicked forward and bent (50 upper,
  -70 lower), arms counter-posed (-60/100) — the speed-kick frame:
  leg out, torso counter-twisted by the arms, not by a redrawn torso.

### GINYU-JEICE — elegant / refined, elbows out, hand near the face
Distinct: the refined one; elbows flared, a hand near the face, poses
that look posed-on-purpose — a fashion-model elegance inside a fight
stance. The signature attack staging is a volleyball-style serve:
turn, raise one hand high, swing the whole body through to smack it.
- JEI-01 crusher serve windup: left hand raised high (-160, top
  layer so it is never buried under the cowl), right hand swinging
  through (80/-110), legs (-25/30) — the serve windup, before the
  swing lands.
- JEI-02 elegant elbows out: elbows flared (130/-130 lower), hands
  near face/chest, legs planted (-20/20), head high (14°) — the
  refined stance, the roll-call pose for this dialect.
- JEI-03 point / smirk side stance: left arm pointed up-out (-110),
  right hand to hip (100), head turned (-12°) — the cocky point:
  "you, over there," as a pose.

### GINYU-GULDO — compact, low, crouch, concentration
Distinct: the smallest member, so the language is LOW and COMPACT:
deep crouches, knees out, hands forward and low, body gathered rather
than spread. Where Recoome spreads, Guldo gathers. The concentration
poses (hands out, head still) are the read for his time power — focus
as a pose, not a blast.
- GUL-01 low crouch compact: deepest squat of the set (-55/55 upper,
  55/-55 lower), hands forward low (90/-90) — the compact base pose,
  knees out / toes out per the knee-feet rule.
- GUL-02 time-freeze focus: arms straight forward out (-90/90,
  30/-30), legs (-40/40), head level — the concentration pose: still,
  gathered, hands out in front of the body.
- GUL-03 guard crouch, startled: guards up near the face (140/-140,
  both arms top-layered so the guards are in front of the face, not
  eaten by it), crouch (-45/45) — the caught-off-guard guard: small,
  low, hands up.

### GINYU-BONYU — FORMER member, pose-refusal (trait-derived, NOT canonical)
Distinct, and the distinction is the point: Bonyu has NO documented
canonical signature pose, because her defining trait is that she
HATES posing and quit over it. Any "Bonyu pose" that pretended to be
canonical would be invented. These three are therefore labelled
trait-derived, built from that one documented trait only:
- BON-01 reluctant roll-call: arms down at the sides (20/-20), legs
  planted, head turned away (-14°) — standing in the roll-call slot
  and refusing to perform it.
- BON-02 walk-off / left the team: stride (-40/20), arms swinging,
  head turned (-10°) — the exit beat: walking out of formation.
- BON-03 training neutral: a plain ready stance (-45/45, 70/-70),
  head level — the only pose she does not refuse: a neutral stance
  with no flourish in it.
If Mars wants a canonical Bonyu pose, that is a Mars-only call — no
source was found that documents one, and none is claimed here.

## 4. Industry-standard staging methods wired in
1. Sentai roll-call timing: announce hold 12f → snap 2f → hold 12f+,
   per member, in canonical order, then a unison final hold.
2. Silhouette test: every pose must read in silhouette alone; checked
   on the contact sheets (grey-128 viewing background only).
3. Contrapposto / S-curve: hips vs shoulders torqued, especially
   Captain and Recoome; never a straight stack.
4. Knee-feet rule (standing): knees-out → toes-out, knees-in →
   toes-in, checked per pose; Guldo's deep crouch is the stress test
   and passes (knees out, toes out).
5. Arm-front-snap + face/cowl snap: a raised arm that crosses the
   face or the cowl is drawn as a WHOLE chain above head/cowl
   (builder `top_arms`); a chest-only crossing arm stays below the
   head. No raised arm is buried under the cowl in this set — that
   was Mars's 18:27 CDT flag on JoJo 03 / Ginyu 01, and it is the
   gate applied here.
6. Hold timing as character: Captain holds longest (centre), Recoome
   performs his holds (dance rhythm), Burter's holds are shortest
   (speed reads as impatience to move), Guldo's holds are still
   (concentration), Bonyu's hold is a refusal (no flourish at all).
7. Staggered-depth composition: in the roll-call strip, the centre
   (Captain) pose is the widest/tallest read; flanks are narrower.
   A solo sequence cannot do true group depth, so the strip orders
   by roll-call order and the final spread pose acts as the unison
   beat.
